# Section Eight: Operations and Logistics — Draft Answers

## 8.1 Customer Journey (Services)

Starting point: what happens when a potential customer makes an enquiry?

- My website is the main channel: customer finds me there (or is directed there via word of mouth/referral) and sees my portfolio and brand before making contact
- Customer submits an enquiry through the website's contact/booking form, or emails me directly
- I reply with an estimate for cost and timescale based on the service they want (real estate, event, social media, or FPV)
- Customer confirms and I arrange a site visit or shoot date, checking airspace and weather in advance
- Customer agrees in writing to the price and date (email confirmation is enough at this stage, a formal contract isn't necessary for Year 1 job sizes)
- I take a 25% deposit to confirm the booking
- I carry out the shoot, edit the footage, and deliver the final files
- Customer pays the remaining balance on delivery

**Note on sequencing:** I'm deliberately not using Instagram or any social presence until my main brand launch, so the website isn't a nice-to-have running alongside social media, it's the thing that has to exist before I can properly take bookings or show my portfolio at all. This makes finishing the website my actual top priority right now, ahead of any other operations work.

## 8.2 Delivery to Customers (Services)

I deliver everything digitally:
- Final photos and video delivered via a shared cloud link (Google Drive or WeTransfer, no extra charge)
- Standard turnaround: 2–3 working days; rush 24-hour delivery available for +£30 (per `real_estate.md`)
- No physical delivery required, keeps my costs and logistics simple in Year 1

## 8.3 Payment Methods and Terms

- Payment methods: bank transfer (primary), possibly a simple invoicing tool like Square or PayPal Invoicing once volume justifies the fees
- 25% deposit to confirm booking, balance due on delivery of final files

- I'll use a basic invoice template (I can generate one) for every job, even paid by bank transfer, so I have a clean paper trail for tax purposes

## 8.7 Transport

- Local jobs (within easy public transport reach) are realistic for Year 1

## 8.8 Legal Requirements

1. **Business registration**: I need to register as a sole trader with HMRC before or shortly after I start trading, and keep basic income/expense records for Self Assessment.
2. **CAA registration**: I need both a Flyer ID (free, valid 5 years) and an Operator ID (currently £12.34/year) before flying commercially, alongside my A2 CofC.
3. **Data protection**: if I store client photos, contact details, or footage of identifiable people, I should check whether I need to register with the ICO, this is likely a low-cost, quick registration for a business my size, but I haven't confirmed the exact fee yet, worth checking gov.uk/data-protection-fee directly.
4. **Terms and conditions**: I need a short T&Cs document covering cancellation, weather rescheduling, and usage rights for delivered footage, before I take my first paid booking.

### Where I'll get legal advice
Free initial guidance via gov.uk (business registration, ICO) and my King's Trust mentor for anything business-structure specific.

## 8.9 Insurance Requirements

This is a genuinely good find for my situation: **FPV UK membership (£24.99/year)** includes £1m commercial public liability cover for up to £15,000 of commercial drone work per year, which comfortably covers my Year 1 turnover target of £12,000 from Section 1.3. That's far cheaper than standalone commercial drone insurance (typically £100–£300/year for similar cover) and matches my exact situation as a new, small-volume sole trader.

| Insurance Type | Supplier | Annual Cost | Notes |
|---|---|---:|---|
| Public Liability (commercial) | FPV UK membership | £24.99 | Covers up to £15k commercial work/year, £1m PL |
| CAA Operator ID | CAA | £12.34 | Required alongside Flyer ID and A2 CofC |

If I outgrow the £15k/year cap, I'll need to move to a standalone commercial drone policy (£100–£300+/year depending on cover level).
