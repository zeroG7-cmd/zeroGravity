# Pricing Strategy

## Starting point: what I actually have
Two real trading data points (Section 4.6):
- 360 Sky Lounge, Nov 2023: £60
- NXP, Sep 2023: £0

Both sit well below sustainable pricing, reasonable for early portfolio-building, but not a basis for my ongoing pricing.

Plus, now, real survey data: 32 responses via Google Forms (Section 5.2), predominantly private individuals (78.1%), with real estate agents underrepresented (3.1%). I'm reading the demand-segment numbers with that composition in mind, they're a strong signal for events/social, and a needs-validation signal (not a rejection) for real estate specifically.

### Key survey findings
- **0%** said they would not consider using my drone services (43.8% Yes, 56.2% Maybe)
- **90.6%** have never used a drone service before, a first-adopter market
- **75.0%** cited Quality as the key purchase factor, ahead of Price and Professionalism (46.9% each), so price isn't my primary lever
- **65.6%** would pay £100 or more (37.5% in £100–200, 28.1% in £200–500)
- **Events (46.9%)** and **Social media content (43.8%)** were the most in-demand use cases, well ahead of real estate (9.4%)

## UK market cross-reference (current, 2026)
- Basic real estate drone photography: **£200–£400** UK average (budget end from ~£100–£275)
- Entry-level drone videography (real estate/event jobs): **£200–£500**
- FPV drone filming: **from £99/hour**, full commercial FPV packages commonly £200–£600+ at entry/mid tier
- Commercial promo video work: **£800–£1,500+** (above my Year 1 target market)

## My Year 1 pricing

| Service | Price | Reasoning |
|---|---|---|
| Real Estate Photography | £150 | Below UK average (£200–400) but not underselling; secondary priority pending agent validation |
| Real Estate Photo + Video | £250 | Low end of entry-level video range (£200–500) |
| Event Coverage (standard) | £200 | Matches entry-level market; my strongest validated demand segment |
| Event Coverage (extended) | £350 | Matches entry-level market upper band |
| Social Media Content Package | £150 | Standalone package now, not just an add-on, reflecting near-equal demand to events |
| FPV Cinematic | £350–500 | Already well-positioned against the £200–600 FPV market |

My survey's own willingness-to-pay bands (37.5% at £100–200, 28.1% at £200–500) line up well with this table, most of these prices sit inside the band most respondents already said they'd accept.

## What this changes about my priority, not just my pricing
Pricing was never really my weak point, my instinct to avoid being cheap was already sound. The bigger finding is where I need to point my marketing effort first: events and social media content have validated demand from my own respondents; real estate doesn't yet, because the people who'd actually buy it (agents) barely got surveyed. That's a marketing sequencing decision for me, not a pricing one, see `events.md` and `real_estate.md` for how that plays out.

## When I'll raise my prices
Two honest triggers, not a calendar date:
1. **Portfolio proof** — once I have 5–10 delivered projects to show, I'll raise Real Estate and Event pricing toward the UK average (£200–400).
2. **Capability proof** — once my Data Collection or FPV/editing output is demonstrably above entry-level, that's when I'll move specialist work past £500 toward the £600–1,000+ mid-tier.

I won't raise prices on confidence alone, I'll raise them when delivered work justifies the new number to a client asking why it went up.
